# fleet design

## What this is and is not

A CLI that runs and coordinates coding agents in herdr; not an agent itself, not a service, not a Go library.

## Model

Three roles. A **thread agent** (`thread-<slug>`, role `thread`) is one conversation on a Slack thread; it starts jobs and is the only agent that talks to people. A **lead** (`<job>-lead`) runs one job and starts workers. A **worker** has the fewest powers: it starts no agent, asks no person itself and ends no job. A lead's worker (`<job>-<name>`) does one task for its lead; a **watch worker** (`watch-<watch>-<key>`; a key not made of `[a-z0-9-]`, or a name past 32 characters, is cut and hashed) is started by `watch` for a subject of a `worker` watch (below), with no job and the parent `watch`, in the scope's `threads` workspace and in `$XDG_STATE_HOME/fleet/<scope>-watch/`, a directory fleet makes outside every checkout. No job name is `watch` or starts with `watch-`, `unblock-` or `revisit-` (the names of the screen helpers and revisit agents `watch` started before its agents were workers), so these names are never those of a job's agents. Each role's first message starts with fleet's built-in text for it (`thread_prompt.md`, `lead_prompt.md`, `worker_prompt.md` in `internal/cmd`, placeholders filled by fleet), then `common_prompt.md`, the text the three share (what the `FLEET_*` variables mean, the trust model, messaging, asking, a failed command, when to stop, the floor), then the additions (`common`'s, then the role's), each as the scope's config makes it (`prompts`, below), so fleet's mechanism reaches the agent with its task. The texts are built in rather than a skill, so they are always there and match the binary's commands. They carry no policy (model tiers, review, merging, releases, what a public repo's text may name, the language of tracker text, the skills to read): that is the config owner's, and reaches agents through the owner's rules, the repo's `AGENTS.md` and `prompts.<name>.append`. Their floor is the same for all: money, machines, risk controls, a release and a change that would stop a running service need a person's yes unless the rules the agent works under allow that case in advance. The config is read at each start, and a running agent keeps the prompt it started with. A thread agent waits for a person on a lead's question only when the rules need a person's yes for it, and hands the rest back to the lead. Thread agents are started by `fleet inbox`, the fednet client's hook, when a message arrives on a thread without a live agent; they run in the herdr workspace `threads` of their scope's session (started by `inbox` through the systemd user unit `fleet-scope@<scope>` when it is not running and the thread gets an agent), one tab per thread next to a `shell` tab that runs no agent (pane `<scope>-shell`; both made or restored before each start), in the directory of the thread's channel (below). A **thread** has at most one live agent and, with a Linear team configured for the scope, one thread ticket for its whole life; each conversation on it is a session, counted on the thread's row, ended by `fleet thread end`. A session lasts while the thread waits on a person or a job (a pending question in it, or an open job whose home thread it is): the answer and the lead's messages reach the live agent, an idle session costs nothing, and a new one starts cold with the prompt, the summaries and the thread.

A **job** is the unit of work, named by its id, with at most one parent issue in Linear. It is a row in the `jobs` table from `job start` until `job end`; its lead and workers are rows in `agents`. A thread agent starts a job, and so may a caller without `FLEET_ROLE` (a timer, a shell) for a single-repo job, under the name `fleet`; such a job has no home thread until its lead first asks a question. A job is single-repo (`--repo`: the lead runs in the main checkout `<checkouts>/<repo>`, or for an initiative's repo `<initiative prefix><I>` with none, in `<initiatives>/<I>` as `fleet worktree` finds it; read-only by convention) or cross-repo (the lead runs in `<job>/` inside the checkout of its home thread's initiative, `<initiatives>/<I>/`, or the general initiative's for a thread of one repo; fleet makes and removes `<job>/`, never the checkout). No command makes a worktree for an agent; agents open theirs with `fleet worktree`, and a worker runs wherever its lead's `--cwd` says.

A **scope** is one fleet: the herdr session `fleet-<scope>`, one ledger and one settings file, isolated from the other scopes on the machine. The caller's scope is `--scope`, else `FLEET_SCOPE`, else `main`; `inbox` takes it from the message (the hub's per-channel `scope`, `main` when absent). The repo of a job is a job attribute, not the ledger key. A scope's own settings (what no repo can hold: the Linear team of thread tickets, the fednet socket, the machine's directories and the channel names) are in `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/fleet/<scope>.json`.

A **channel** decides where a thread belongs (its *mapping*), by the scope's settings (below): `<repo prefix><R>` runs its thread agents in the main checkout `<checkouts>/<R>`; `<initiative prefix><I>` in `<initiatives>/<I>/`, the checkout of the initiative's repo, which has the channel's name; a direct message to the bot belongs to the general initiative, `<initiative prefix><general initiative>`. fleet makes none of these checkouts. Any other channel is ignored. A thread keeps the mapping and directory recorded at its first delivery, but its kind (repo or initiative) is read with the current prefixes, so `channels` is changed only while no thread is recorded.

## Parts and how they connect

- `cmd/fleet`: parses the command line (standard `flag`, one `FlagSet` per command, `--scope` global, `job` (with `start`, `list` and `end`) and `thread` command groups) and dispatches to `internal/cmd`; turns a failure into `fleet: <message>` on stderr and the exit code.
- `internal/cmd`: one file per command, plus `agent.go` with what the two starters share (names, the task file, the config and Linear settings of a job, the work order, places in herdr, starting Claude Code and getting it past the folder-trust dialog to its input box: only the dialog for the agent's own directory, one key per press from where the cursor is among the dialog's options on the screen just read, not on the shell's command line above it; a start that fails with herdr's `pane get` showing no agent in the pane (Claude Code exited, as a PATH shim does while an update reinstalls it) is made once more in the same pane after 3 s, a slow one still in the pane is not; delivery, the machine's resources). `job start` runs its checks, does the Linear steps (parent created with `--new-parent`, parent claimed for the lead, lead's work order created and claimed), writes the job row and the lead's `starting` row, brings the main checkout the lead reads (the repo's, or the initiative's of a cross-repo job) up to origin (below), makes the cross-repo job's directory and the workspace, starts the lead and delivers its first message: the lead prompt, `## Note from fleet` when that checkout was left as it was or the start is `--light`, the latest message a person posted in the home thread (from the thread's row, as one JSON line with its sender and time) and the task. `spawn` (lead only) does the same for a worker in a tab of the job's workspace, with the job's parent issue from the jobs row, its first message the worker prompt, `## Note from fleet` with `--light`, and the task. `send` adds the `[FROM: <agent>]` header and delivers through `herdr.Prompt`; a thread agent's message that reaches a lead marks that lead's questions pending in the thread agent's thread answered, but those a pending card of the thread agent's asks the people for. `done` (workers only) delivers the report to the lead and ends the caller's row only when herdr reports it delivered; with `FLEET_ISSUE` it first writes the report to that issue and releases it through `internal/atb`. `worktree` opens `<worktrees>/<repo>/<job>[-<name>]` from origin/HEAD, or with `--detach <ref>` as a detached checkout of that commit (for a review of a PR head), and records it in the scope's ledger. `job end` (the lead, from its own pane; refused while the job has live workers) writes the report to the lead's work order and releases it, writes the conclusion to the parent issue and releases it, closes the other agents of the job's workspace, refuses while any agent but the lead has its cwd inside a recorded worktree or the cross-repo directory, removes them, ends the job with its outcome and the lead's row, hands the conclusion to `concluded` (the home-thread hook; it prints until thread agents exist) and closes the workspace last. `job end <job> --force` (a thread agent, or no `FLEET_ROLE`) reclaims a job from outside: no report, no Linear step; it closes the workspace first, cleans up with the same checks, and ends the job as `abandoned` with its live rows. `status` prints the open jobs as `job list` does, then the live rows joined with `herdr agent list`. `report` reads only the ledger's `jobs`, `threads` and `questions`. `job list` joins the open jobs with their live lead and workers. `watch` (`watch.go`, `watchthread.go`, `watches.go`; for a timer, one run per scope at a time under a lock file next to the ledger, starting nothing new after 45 s) reads everything first (herdr, the screens, the config files, with a socket each relevant thread's newest message through `fednet client read-thread`; the keys of the config it no longer uses are named on stderr), a failed read doing nothing (but a thread fednet answers it cannot give, its exit 1 or 2, only skips that thread's rules, saying so, exit 5), then acts, sending as `watch` through `send`'s path. Jobs: each live lead's and job worker's herdr status, `state_change_seq` and a hash of its screen with Claude Code's spinner, input box and footer stripped are recorded; one unchanged for the job's `worker_stale` (or gone from herdr: a `starting` row only 10 minutes after its start, since a start writes the row before herdr has the agent), and not blocked, is a suspect; every suspect is printed, and by the watch `stale-workers` (below) each lead is told only when the set of suspects among its workers changes; a lead's own state goes into the quiet-thread notice. Workers: then `watchworkers.go` closes the watch workers that are done (below). Screens: then `unblock.go` looks at the agents stopped at a screen (below). Threads, with a socket: a live thread agent gone from herdr has its session ended as broken off (the claim its session took released `--restore`, the ticket back to its state before that claim, or, atb refusing that with exit 1, released keeping the state); a thread idle for `thread_idle` has its session reclaimed (the agent asked to end, then after 10 minutes ended by `thread end`'s recorded steps with a comment that no summary was left); by the watch `quiet-threads` a thread with no pending question quiet for `thread_quiet` is asked about once per quiet spell (the newest message's ts kept in `threads.quiet_asked`): with open jobs reporting to it, its thread agent, started through `route` when none is live, gets each job's state; with none, a live agent is asked why it has not ended. Questions: one post reminds a thread of its pending questions (a card by its link; a snoozed card not until it is back, its reminders and timeout then counted from `snooze_until`) but the leads' (which went to the thread agent, never to the people; the live thread agent, unless being reclaimed, gets one message listing them, but those a pending card asks the people for, at the same steps, counted only when not surely undelivered (blocked, not found), a moved one's marked as its job's ended, none being started for it) at each of `reminders` after the oldest (the count and the post's msg_id on the questions), mentioning `threads.last_user` (`-mention`; none when empty, and posted again without it when fednet refuses the mention: exit 2 from a fednet too old for it, 3 for a user not on the hub's list), and replaces the reminder before it: once the new one is posted the older ones are `stale` and deleted (`fednet client delete`), exit 1 (gone) counting as deleted, 4 and 5 (hub unreachable, not in Slack yet) or a fednet that did not run tried again next run, any other exit (a hub too old for it) leaving it `kept`, each failure said on stderr without stopping the other rules; a thread agent's question pending for `thread_question` is closed and its session reclaimed; a lead's pending for `lead_question` gets the lead told, and 30 minutes later the job, if still open with the question pending, is reclaimed as `job end --force` does (`reclaimJob`) and the Linear steps not done are posted to the thread, its pending questions closed; so are those of a job that ended after its lead was told (`jobs.reclaim_at` set), by the lead or by a run that stopped before closing them, and a screen question of any job that ended, its agent gone with the job. A lead's question still pending when its job ended any other way (`job end`, `job end --force`) is moved to the home thread, keeping its row (`job`, `asked_by`, `asked_at`, the reminder count): a question belongs to the open job of its name only when asked after that job started, so a later job of the same name does not take it. It is the thread's from then on: its thread agent reminded of it as of any lead's question, it keeps `thread end` waiting, and the live thread agent, unless being reclaimed, is told of it once (step `watch-moved:<id>`), none being started for it; it is closed after `thread_question` from when it was asked, without reclaiming the session, which may be doing other work.
- `internal/cmd/inbox.go` and `thread.go`: `inbox` reads the event file, finds the thread's mapping from `trigger` and `channel_name` (an ignored channel opens no ledger unless the scope's ledger already knows the thread), reserves the `msg_id` in the scope's ledger (a delivered or dropped message exits 0 at once), and routes: a live thread agent gets the message through `Deliver` headed `[FROM: inbox]` (a person's message marks answered only the thread's pending questions fleet posted as text: a watch worker's, a screen's, watch's own at a `max`); a message delivered or given to a new agent is recorded on the thread's row as the latest from a person (`last_text`, `last_user`, `last_ts`); a live row whose agent herdr no longer has is ended; otherwise, when the thread's directory exists (else one line is posted and the message dropped, as for Linear below), `threadStart` reserves the agent's row and counts the session, does the Linear steps (new thread: ticket created with `atb linear create --label thread` and claimed; known thread: claimed again, a `Session <n> started` comment, the `Session <n> ended` comments read with `atb linear query`), brings the thread's directory up to origin, makes the tab in the `threads` workspace (the workspace when missing), starts Claude Code as `spawn` does and delivers the embedded prompt (`thread_prompt.md`) with the channel context, the summaries, `## Note from fleet` (what Linear did not do, a checkout left as it was) and the message. Linear (`linear.go`): a step atb fails is tried twice more after 3 s and 7 s, but not a call that timed out or could not run, nor a claim another agent holds (atb's exit 3), and no try begins, nor a pause is waited out, once the steps' shared 90 s are spent (`LinearBudget`), so, a call taking at most atb's 60 s, the Linear part of a start ends within 2.5 minutes; each failed try is a JSON line in `<scope>-linear.log` (below). A step that still fails ends the Linear steps and the agent starts anyway: after the claim it keeps the ticket and is told what is missing; before it (the ticket not created, Linear unavailable, or the claim held) the session runs without its ticket (`FLEET_ISSUE` empty, the row's `issue` too), the thread is told in one line, as far as fednet and the socket allow, naming the step and atb's exit or the holder, and the message is delivered as usual. The next session creates or claims the ticket as it would anyway; `thread end` of a session without its ticket in a scope with a Linear team first keeps the summary on the machine (`<scope>-summaries/<slug>/<session>.md` beside the ledger, 0600, step `keep`), then writes the thread's kept summaries to its ticket, creating the ticket (unclaimed) when there is none (step `create`), each file removed once Linear took it; what Linear does not take stays kept, the ending goes on, and the next start that claims the ticket writes the kept summaries, oldest first, before its `Session <n> started` comment; `watch` reclaims such a session with no Linear step, since the agent's row has no ticket. A main checkout (a thread's directory, a lead's) is changed only by `git merge --ff-only --no-overwrite-ignore` after `git fetch origin` (30 s), when it is on origin/HEAD's branch, has no change to a tracked file, and is only behind, so an untracked or ignored file the update would bring in stops it; otherwise, and when the fetch fails, it is left, the start goes on, and the agent's first message says why (git's own output left out). `thread end` is refused while the thread waits (its pending questions and the open jobs it is home to, listed) unless `--asked-to-end`; it then writes the summary comment, releases the ticket, posts the closing line (`会话已结束 · <ticket>`, a small grey `-footer` post that also completes an open progress card; the thread and ticket come from the ledger rows, not the environment), ends the row and closes the tab last, each step recorded through `runStep` (`steps.go`) so a retry continues and the line is posted at most once; `thread post` posts a thread agent's text to the thread its live row records, through the scope's socket; `thread progress` sends the agent's whole progress card (or `--done`) the same way, keeping no card state (the hub tracks the one open card per thread and closes it before any post); `thread set-project` and `thread relate` act on `FLEET_ISSUE`. The embedded prompt names neither the thread key nor the socket: thread agents post only through fleet; it tells them to post one line on a person's message, keep a card while they work, and post the reply; the session summary has a section on what the people decided or corrected, and a job's task file carries what bears on it.
- `internal/cmd/askhuman.go`: a question for the people is always a card, and only a thread agent posts one. `ask-thread` (leads) records a lead's card as pending (table `questions`; the same card asked again while pending is the same row) and takes it to the job's home thread through `route` as `[QUESTION #<id> from <lead>]` and the card's JSON, so a thread agent is started for the thread when none is live; fleet posts none of it: the thread agent answers it with `send`, looks it up, or asks the people with `ask-human --card --for <id>`. The delivery is a step recorded under `ask-human:<question id>`, so a retry continues and never delivers twice. A single-repo job without a home thread gets one at the first ask, before the question is recorded: `fednet client open-thread` in the channel of its checkout (`<repo prefix><R>`, or the initiative's channel for an initiative's checkout), or in the general initiative's when fleet knows no id of that channel, the channel's id taken from the newest thread of it in `threads`, or else from the route `fednet client routes -json` gives with the channel's name, read once per open (direct messages, the `dm` route and ids starting `D`, left out; routes fednet cannot give name no channel, said on stderr); the thread opens with fleet's line naming the job and its repo (fednet opens no thread without a message), never the question; the new thread is written as a `threads` row of that mapping and as `jobs.home_thread` in one transaction. `ask-human --card` (thread agents) records the card and posts it with `fednet client ask -ref <scope>:<id>`, the ref carrying the scope since one fednet client serves the machine's scopes and an answer carries nothing but the ref; fednet alone defines and checks the card (its fields and limits: `fednet client ask --help`; the shapes fleet's agents write and receive are in `fleet ask-human --help`), and posts no second card for a ref, so a retry after a failure is safe; a card fednet refuses (exit 2) posted nothing and its question is closed. `--close` takes a card back (`fednet client ask-close`). `ask-human --file`, deprecated, keeps an older prompt's agents asking: a lead's text goes on as `ask-thread`, a thread agent's is posted as a card with no options. `closeCards` takes back, warning on failure, the card of every question fleet closes and the pending cards asked for it. `concluded` (`jobend.go`, the `conclude` step of `job end`) takes the job's conclusion to the home thread the same way, marked as a conclusion that needs no answer; a job without a home thread only prints it, and a delivery that did not succeed fails the step so a retry delivers again.
- `internal/cmd/watchworkers.go` (run by `watch`): a watch worker is started in a new tab of the `threads` workspace, its first message, headed `[FROM: watch]`, the worker text (`common` and the additions as for a lead's worker; its slots filled for no job, the lead `watch`), `## Earlier` when earlier workers of the subject asked (their questions with their state, and the latest message a person posted in the thread of the last), and `## Your task`: `fleet watch, watch `<name>`, subject `<key>`; your question file is <dir>/<worker>-question.md:`, the subject's text and the watch's prompt (slots `{{key}}`, `{{text}}`, `{{question_file}}`). Its row's `task` is the subject as JSON (`watch`, `key`, `job`, `thread`, `agent`). Each run first closes the live watch workers (and the screen helpers and revisit agents of before, their files in `<scope>-unblock/`): one done with its turn (idle or done), blocked itself, working for 30 minutes, gone, or left `starting` by a failed start is closed (its tab, then its row), after the question it wrote, if any, is asked: for the `unblock` watch about the stopped agent (below); else in the subject's thread (its own, else its job's home thread, else its agent's; a revisit agent's in the home thread of the latest job on its issue that has one) as a question pending from the worker (reminded of, never timed out, closed when its job ends) and given to the thread's agent as there being nothing to pass on; without a thread or a socket it goes to stderr.
- `internal/cmd/unblock.go` (run by `watch`): an agent of a job or a thread agent is stopped at a screen when herdr reports an active one `blocked`, or a `starting` row's start (exit 3) left it at a screen for 10 minutes (sooner, the start may still be running). Each stopped agent without a live worker (`watch-unblock-<row id>`, or a helper `unblock-<row id>` of before): nothing while a question about it (asked by either name) is pending; the rules (`classifyScreen`), built in whatever the watches say: Claude Code's folder-trust dialog for the agent's own directory is answered by the same code as a start (`settle`), nobody told (when it does not get the agent to its input box, the next run looks again); a screen whose dialog part (below the last horizontal rule) names a usage limit, a model switch, a usage reset or a purchase gets no key and no worker, and the people are asked once (step `ask-hands-off` of `unblock:<row id>`); any other screen, when the default watch `unblock` (source `blocked-screens`, `worker`) is due, gets a new worker, the 4th time the people are asked once instead (`ask-helpers`). Its subject is the stopped agent, how to read and press on it, the ticket and the screen; its prompt is `unblock_prompt.md` and the guidance (`screen_guidance.md`), embedded, so it is the binary's own, unless the entry's `prompt` takes the place of both. It reads and presses keys on the stopped agent only, with `herdr agent read` and `send-keys`, one key at a time, never text. Covered by the guidance, it presses what the guidance says (for a command Claude Code flags as possibly unsafe or could not check, with a countdown to deny it, nothing: the denial lets that agent go on) and records on the ticket; not covered, it presses nothing and writes its question to its question file, which the next run asks when it closes the worker; once the people answer, the next worker gets the answer and records on the ticket their decision and the guidance entry it suggests (a person or a reviewing agent changes the guidance by PR). Asking is a post to the stopped agent's home thread (its own thread for a thread agent, else its job's, opened first as `ask-thread` opens it for a single-repo job without one) with `fednet client post`, a `questions` row pending from the worker (the question rules remind of it, but never time it out: it waits until a person answers in the thread, or is closed when the stopped agent's job ends), and, unless the stopped agent is that thread's own, a delivery to the thread's agent as `ask-thread` delivers, saying there is nothing to pass on. The ticket, with `linear.team` and `unblock.project` configured, is created by fleet before the first worker of a row (`atb linear create --label blocked-screen`, not claimed), is the worker's `FLEET_ISSUE`, and is reused by the next ones; a failed create starts no worker this run. A worker's lead is told when a worker starts or the people are asked about the worker; the starter of a lead or worker still `starting` (its parent) is told also when the rule answered, since the agent may not have its first message; a thread agent's start is finished by `inbox` with the thread's next message.
- `internal/cmd/watches.go` (run by `watch`): the scope's `watches`, in name order. Three are defaults, there without the key: `stale-workers` (source `stale-agents`, the lead rule of Jobs above), `quiet-threads` (source `quiet-threads`, the quiet rule of Threads) and `unblock` (source `blocked-screens`, the workers of Screens); each message is its facts, then the watch's prompt (built in: `watch_stale_workers.md`, `watch_quiet_threads.md`, and `unblock_prompt.md` with the guidance, so what a thread agent does about a quiet thread comes with the notice, not from the thread prompt). An entry of a default's name sets only its `every`, `prompt` and `off`; no other entry may name a built-in source, since each keeps its state in the ledger's own columns and rows (`agents.suspect`, `threads.quiet_asked`, the workers' rows and questions by name with the `unblock:<row id>` steps). Every other entry has a command source (a `worker` entry may have none: a timer whose one subject, its own name, has a new text each run), run after the thread rules while the run has time: its program through herdr's runner with a 30 s deadline and, beyond the allow-list, only the variables `env` names (the values from fleet's own environment); stdin one JSON object (`version` 1, `scope`, `now`, `jobs`: the scope's jobs, open and ended, in start order, each with `job`, `parent_issue`, `repo`, `home_thread`, `state`, `outcome`, `started_at`, `ended_at`, null while open); stdout one JSON object per line, one per subject, another key refused: `key` (unique in the output), `text`, and the `agent`, `job` or `thread` it concerns, one at least for `tell`. A source that exits non-zero (the first line of its stderr shown, at most 200 bytes, the values of its `env` variables taken out before the cut), times out, prints more than 4 MiB or prints anything else skips its watch for the run, saying so, exit 5. A subject is due when first seen, then, with `again`, once that long passed since its last act, else when the digest of its text changed; at most `per_run` due subjects a run get an act, in output order. `do: tell` sends `fleet watch, watch `<name>`, subject `<key>`:`, the text and the prompt (slots `{{key}}`, `{{text}}`), headed `[FROM: watch]`, to the agent named, else the job's live lead (none: exit 4), else the thread's agent through `route`; only a tell that landed (exit 0 or 2) counts as an act, the others are tried next run. `do: worker` starts a watch worker for the subject (above; `model` and `effort` the entry's), counted as an act once it has its first message; none while the subject's worker is live or its question is pending, and once a person answered it after the last start (both times the ledger's clock), one whatever the text and `again` say. A subject due again whose acts reached `max` gets instead, once, a question posted to its thread (its own, else its job's home thread, else its agent's), pending from `watch` (reminded of, never timed out, closed when its job ends), the thread's agent told there is nothing to pass on; without a thread or a socket the question goes to stderr. Either way nothing more is done about it while its source gives it; a subject the output no longer has is dropped. A watch with `every` records its last run as step `ran` of `watch:<name>` (the run's clock), unless the run ran out of time or a broken prompt stopped the watch, so what it did not get to waits only for the next run. A broken prompt file skips the watch for the run, posted once to the first thread it would have reached, as for the screen helpers.
- `internal/herdr`: runs the `herdr` CLI with a bounded environment and a deadline, parses its one-object JSON reply, and maps `agent prompt` outcomes to the exit codes.
- `internal/atb`: runs `atb linear create` (only an identifier and an https URL are taken from its `--json` output; a thread ticket is labelled `thread`, a lead's work order `lead`, a worker's `worker`), `claim`, `comment`, `release`, `set-project`, `relate` and `query` (a parent's team and project, a thread's `Session <n> ended` comments) through herdr's runner (deadline, process group, allow-list plus `LINEAR_API_KEY`, `LINEAR_API_KEY_CMD`, `LINEAR_API_URL`, `ATB_HOME`); the key travels only in the environment, and atb's output never goes into an error; a failure keeps atb's exit and its stderr with credentials redacted, which `fleet` prints on its stderr after the error.
- `internal/fednet`: runs `fednet client post`, `client progress`, `client ask` and `client ask-close` through the same runner: what fleet itself says in a thread, including the closing line (`post -footer`; fednet's output hidden), and `thread post` and `thread progress` (fednet's output and exit code handed back).
- `internal/config`: a repo's `.fleet/config.json`, read from its checkout, and a scope's `<scope>.json`, with the defaults of its paths and channels; standard library only. Unknown keys and wrong types are refused, naming the key.
- `internal/db`: the ledger (`modernc.org/sqlite`, WAL, 5 s busy timeout, immediate transactions) and its schema migration.
- `internal/identity`: the caller's identity from the `FLEET_*` variables; `FLEET_THREAD` is injected only into a thread agent's pane.
- `internal/exit`: the exit-code contract and the failure type every command returns.
- `internal/cliargs`: the clap-like behaviors `flag` lacks (flags after positionals, a string flag that records whether it was given).
- Releases: the version is `const version` in `cmd/fleet/main.go`, and a release is a `v<version>` tag pushed to `Luolc/agent-fleet`, from which `.github/workflows/release.yml` creates the GitHub Release. ## Invariants

Each invariant links to the test that guards it, or is marked untested.

- The CLI behaves as the judge (`judge/`) says: argv, exit codes, stdout/stderr, ledger rows, herdr state and screens. Guarded by `judge/run.sh`; every command's cases are in `judge/usage.sh`, `send.sh`, `lifecycle.sh`, `watch.sh`, `worktree.sh`, `thread.sh`, `watchthread.sh`, `watches.sh` and `unblock.sh`.
- `inbox` is idempotent by `msg_id`: the message is reserved before anything else, a message once delivered or dropped is never acted on again, and a rerun while the thread's agent row is live (a run killed after the start) delivers to that agent instead of starting another; one live thread agent per thread is a partial unique index. Guarded by `cmd/fleet/thread_test.go`, `internal/db/db_test.go` and `judge/thread.sh`.
- An ending (`thread end`) can always be finished: a retry skips the recorded steps and continues, so the closing line goes to the thread at most once, and `--force` finishes the local cleanup when Linear or the post keeps failing, naming what is left. Guarded by `cmd/fleet/thread_test.go` and `judge/thread.sh`.
- A thread agent's session is not ended while the thread waits on a person or a job, unless the agent says the people asked it to end (`--asked-to-end`) or `watch` reclaims it. Guarded by `cmd/fleet/thread_test.go` and `judge/thread.sh`.
- A Linear step failing in `inbox` never drops the message or stops the start: the agent starts with what Linear did and is told the rest, each failed try logged with atb's exit and its stderr, credentials redacted by shape and by `LINEAR_API_KEY`'s value, never in an error, a post or a prompt. Guarded by `cmd/fleet/thread_test.go`, `internal/cmd/linear_test.go`, `internal/atb/atb_test.go` and `judge/thread.sh`.
- A start changes a main checkout only by fast-forwarding a clean checkout on origin/HEAD's branch that is only behind, never overwriting an untracked or ignored file, and never fails for it. A session's summary that Linear did not take at `thread end` is kept until a later start or ending writes it. Guarded by `internal/cmd/checkout_test.go` and `judge/thread.sh`.
- A start reserves its ledger rows first, in one immediate transaction with the dedup checks (`job start`: the job row and the lead's `starting` row; `spawn`: the worker's `starting` row), then does the Linear steps (`job start`: parent, parent claim, work order, claim; `spawn`: work order, claim), and creates nothing else before they are done; an identifier is written onto the rows as soon as its issue exists, before the claim, so a failure after the reservation lists the rows, which keep the identifiers written so far, and `fleet job end <job> --force`; except that `spawn` ends its worker's row when a Linear step fails, since nothing is in herdr yet and no claim is held, so the name is free and the lead's `job end` is not held up; the key never appears in an argument, output or error, and atb's stderr is shown only redacted. Guarded by `cmd/fleet/workorder_test.go`.
- One live lead per parent issue, one open job per name and per key in a scope: two starts cannot both pass, even concurrently, because the reservation serializes them and the partial unique indexes refuse what slips through; a parent created by `--new-parent` is bound to the rows before anything else happens; the second is refused naming the first, and a parent whose lead ended is reused. Guarded by `cmd/fleet/workorder_test.go` (a start held at its parent claim, given or just created, while another is refused) and `internal/db/db_test.go` (the indexes).
- Every refusal of `job start` and `spawn` happens before any row is written, any Linear issue created or claimed, and anything made in herdr; the checks before the reservation read only (`herdr agent list`, `herdr workspace list`, `atb linear query`). Guarded by `cmd/fleet/lifecycle_test.go`.
- `--light` (`job start`, `spawn`) skips only the load check; memory, the cap and every other check still run. The agent's first message tells it to only read and do I/O, and to stop and say so before a build, a test suite or anything else CPU-heavy: a promise in the prompt that fleet does not enforce. The row's `light` records it and `status` flags it. Guarded by `internal/cmd/agent_test.go` (the check), `cmd/fleet/workorder_test.go` (the row and the note) and `cmd/fleet/status_test.go`; that `spawn` and `job start` pass the flag to the check is untested, since a test cannot fake the load of the binary it runs.
- `job end` removes nothing an agent (other than the lead ending its job) is still in, and never a worktree with uncommitted changes (its submodules' included, and a submodule's stashes and commits on no remote) or a cross-repo directory holding a git checkout with uncommitted changes; a lead's `job end` changes nothing in herdr, and nothing in the ledger but the record of its completed Linear steps, before those steps succeeded; a retry after a failure skips the recorded steps (`runStep`), never repeating a release, and only the record counts as done (atb's refusal of an issue nobody holds is a failure); a lead whose ending marked the job ended but failed to close the workspace may run `job end` again to finish. `job end --force` finishes the local cleanup whatever Linear did, and prints the Linear steps of the open job's ending that are not recorded. Guarded by `cmd/fleet/worktree_test.go` and `lifecycle_test.go` (a stateful retry).
- `watch` tells a lead only about its own workers, and only when their suspect set changes; a blocked or missing lead is told again next run; an agent blocked at a prompt is never a suspect. Guarded by `cmd/fleet/watch_test.go` and `judge/watch.sh`.
- Without `watches`, `watch` tells and asks as before the key existed: `judge/watch.sh` and `watchthread.sh` were not changed for it. A command watch acts on a subject only when it is due, at most `per_run` a run, counts only a tell that landed or a worker that has its first message, asks the people about a subject once at its `max` and then leaves it, and a source that fails skips only its own watch. A `worker` watch has at most one live worker per subject and starts none while that worker's question waits, which never times out nor gets the thread's session reclaimed; once the people answered, the next run starts one with their answer. A watch worker is never a suspect of the job rules. Guarded by `cmd/fleet/watches_test.go`, `internal/cmd/watchworkers_test.go`, `internal/config/config_test.go` and `judge/watches.sh`.
- Only a press answers a card: a person's message answers only the questions fleet posted as text, and a thread agent's `send` to a lead does not answer a lead's question a pending card asks the people for. An answer reaches the thread agent, and the lead it was asked for, each under its own question id, at most once per msg_id; a press on a question fleet closed is recorded and delivered to nobody, and fleet takes back the card of each question it closes. A card is asked with the ref `<scope>:<id>`, so a retry posts no second card. Guarded by `cmd/fleet/askhuman_test.go` and `cmd/fleet/watch_test.go`.
- `watch` acts only on a run that read everything, but for the threads fednet answered it cannot give, which it skips; it asks a thread's agent about a quiet spell once, closes a lead's pending question only when watch ended its job by force or the job ended after its lead was told (any other ending moves it to the home thread, its session kept), reminds of a thread's questions at most once per step of `reminders` in one post (the leads' in one message to its agent), deleting the reminders it replaces only after it is posted, and ends a session or a job only after telling its agent and waiting out the grace (10 minutes, 30 minutes). Guarded by `cmd/fleet/watch_test.go` and `judge/watchthread.sh`.
- `watch` sends no text to any agent's input box and presses keys only on a stopped agent's screen: by itself only at the folder-trust dialog for the agent's own directory; never on a usage limit, model switch, usage reset or purchase screen, which no worker gets either. A stopped agent has at most one live worker (the `unblock` watch's, or a helper of before), at most 3 in all, and none while a question about it waits for the people; each of the two asks fleet makes about it on its own is made once. The folder-trust rule and those asks hold with the `unblock` watch off. Guarded by `internal/cmd/unblock_test.go`, `internal/cmd/watchworkers_test.go`, `cmd/fleet/watches_test.go` and `judge/unblock.sh`.
- `watch` reads nothing from Linear: the parent-issue rule is a scope's own command watch, if any. A revisit agent or screen helper live at the upgrade is closed by the watch workers' rules, its question read where it wrote it. Guarded by `cmd/fleet/watch_test.go` and `cmd/fleet/watches_test.go`.
- A start presses keys only at Claude Code's folder-trust dialog, and only when it names the agent's own directory; the dialog for another directory and every other screen stop the start with exit 3 and the screen. A start (or the finishing of an interrupted one) is made at most twice, in the same pane under the same name, so a second start never runs beside a live agent and the ledger keeps one row. Guarded by `internal/cmd/agent_test.go` and `judge/lifecycle.sh`.
- The built-in prompts as fleet fills them in for a thread agent, a lead, a worker and the `unblock` watch's worker (its screen guidance included), rendered from fixed contexts with no `prompts` or `models` in the scope's config, are byte for byte the files in `internal/cmd/testdata/prompts`; only `go test ./internal/cmd -run Golden -update` rewrites them, so a change to a prompt shows as a diff of those files. An addition changes the same contexts' texts by exactly the addition; `models` change none of them. Guarded by `internal/cmd/prompt_golden_test.go` and `internal/cmd/prompts_test.go`.
- A prompt file the scope's config names that cannot be used (missing, unreadable, empty, or with a slot its text does not have) is refused where an agent needing it starts, before anything is made for it: `job start` and `spawn` before any row, Linear issue or herdr change; `inbox` before the reservation and Linear; `watch` before a worker's ticket or row. Guarded by `internal/cmd/prompts_test.go`, `cmd/fleet/lifecycle_test.go`, `judge/lifecycle.sh`, `judge/thread.sh` and `judge/unblock.sh`.

- `ask-human --card <file> [--for <id>]` and `ask-human --close <id> [--note <text>]` (thread agents with `FLEET_THREAD`): `--card` records the card pending (the same card pending again is the same row) and, unless its card is posted, runs `fednet client ask -socket <socket> -thread <FLEET_THREAD> -ref <scope>:<id> -card <file> -json`, recording `id` and `permalink`, and prints `asked question #<id> in thread <key>: <permalink>`; `--for` must name a lead's question pending in the thread. `--close` runs `fednet client ask-close -socket <socket> [-note <text>] -- <card_id>` on a card of the caller's pending in the thread (a card not posted yet is only closed) and closes it as `withdrawn`. A cross-repo job reads the same file in its initiative checkout (the parent of the lead's directory), refused the same way, but not its `linear`: Linear is on exactly when the job has a parent issue, whose team and project come from `atb linear query` (an issue without a project is refused).
- `job start <job> --task-file <file> [--parent-issue <ISSUE> | --new-parent <TITLE>] [--repo <repo>] [--key <key>] [--model] [--effort]` (default `models.lead`), thread agents, or a caller without `FLEET_ROLE` with `--repo` (the lead's `FLEET_PARENT` and first message's sender are then `fleet`, the job has no home thread, and `## Latest message from a person in the home thread` says so and names the channel `ask-thread` will open one in); the job name `threads` is reserved, and so are `watch` and the names starting `watch-`, `unblock-` or `revisit-`. The caller's `FLEET_THREAD` becomes the job's home thread, and with Linear on its thread ticket (`FLEET_ISSUE`) is related to the parent after the work order (`atb linear relate`). With Linear on exactly one parent flag is required; `--new-parent` is refused for a cross-repo job; with Linear off both are refused. The job row and the lead's row are reserved first; then the Linear steps in order: `atb linear create --team --project --title <TITLE> --description-file <task> --json` (only with `--new-parent`), `atb linear claim <parent> --agent <job>-lead --source <caller> --scope '<repo or cross-repo>: job <job>'`, `atb linear create ... --parent <parent> --label lead --title <first non-empty task line without leading #, at most 80 characters> ...`, `atb linear claim <new> ...`. Then the lead's row gets the work order, `<job>/` is made in the cross-repo root (above; its checkout must exist, else exit 1 before anything) for a cross-repo job, a workspace labelled `<job>` whose first tab is `lead`, Claude Code with the fixed arguments, and the first message delivered headed `[FROM: <caller>]`: the lead prompt (identity, where it runs, the job's Linear state, the agent cap, `FLEET_SCRATCH` for its files, which part of the message speaks for a person, how to start workers (an implementer in a worktree the lead opens first) and what a task file carries (the people's decisions and corrections, routes ruled out, what must hold before the next step, when to stop), follow them and change route after two reports without progress, finish what is inside the goal before ending, decide on and make merges itself after an LGTM, read from the reviewer's verdict comment on the PR rather than the message, with `--match-head-commit`, once the PR's acceptance record and the reviewer's failing-test line check out against the approved head (and the required check is green, where the repo's AGENTS.md keeps one), then watch the merge commit's run on main and get a red one fixed or reverted, find out why a command failed before asking, look for an earlier answer before asking the people, ask them and end), `## Latest message from a person in the home thread` (one JSON object `{"user", "ts", "text"}` from the thread's row, the text as written, so no newline or heading in it ends the section; or a line saying none is recorded), and `## Your task` (`Work order: <url>` when the lead has one, then the task file as it is, to the end: fleet places the heading, so the task cannot move where it begins, and a record-shaped block inside it stays inside it). Refused (exit 1) when an open job has the name or the key, a live lead has the parent issue, the lead's name is live or in herdr, a workspace has the label, or the cross-repo directory exists; the message names what is in the way. An atb step exiting non-zero is exit 5 with what was created listed, the reserved rows included; on any failure after the reservation the output lists what exists and `fleet job end <job> --force`.
- `spawn <name> --task-file <file> --cwd <dir> [--model] [--effort]` (default `models.worker`), leads only: the worker `<job>-<name>` in a new tab of the job's workspace, in `--cwd` (an existing directory). The worker's row is reserved first (in one transaction with the name and cap checks); with Linear on the work order then goes under the job's parent issue as above with `--label worker`, `--scope '<repo or cross-repo>: job <job>'`; a single-repo job with Linear on and no parent issue is refused. When a Linear step fails, the row is ended (the work order it keeps, if created, listed as claimed by nobody) and nothing else is created. The cap counts live rows of the job including the lead. The first message, headed `[FROM: <lead>]`, is the worker prompt (identity, where it runs, messages and their exit codes, the report file, what an implementer (an acceptance record in the PR: what ran, at which head, its output line, what did not run), a reviewer (revert the change and show the new test fail) and a researcher do, that merging is the lead's, when to stop and report, which steps need a person's yes through the lead, finding out why a command failed before asking, `done` (the report also names the routes ruled out and what must hold before the next step), and pushing or saving as a patch what is worth keeping before `--abandon`) and `## Your task` as for a lead.
- `job list [--all] [--json]`: the open jobs of the caller's scope whose home thread has the caller's mapping (a thread agent's own thread, a lead's or worker's job's home thread), or all of them with `--all` or for a caller with no thread; one line per job, oldest first, tab-separated `job`, `parent_issue`, `key`, `repo`, `lead`, `workers` (comma-joined live workers); `-` for an empty field; nothing when no job is open. `--json`: objects with `job`, `parent_issue`, `key`, `repo`, `lead_cwd`, `lead` (null when none is live), `workers`, `started_at`.
- `report [--json]`: read-only, from the ledger alone. One line per job, open and ended, in start order, with its state (`open`, its outcome, or `unknown` for a question whose job has no row), the jobs that asked nothing included; one line per thread for the questions its thread agent asked (`job` empty), only for threads with such a question; then the total. Each line: questions asked, answered and pending, and the median and longest wait of the answered ones (`answered_at - asked_at`). A question counts for the latest job of its name started by the time it was asked, since a name is unique only among open jobs; a rerun of `ask-human` or `ask-thread` while its question is pending adds no row. `answered_at` is when a person submitted or closed the card, when the thread agent's `send` reached the lead for a lead's question, or, for a question fleet posted as text, when `inbox` took the next message a person posted in the thread, which marks every such question pending there answered: the wait runs to that message, not necessarily to the answer. `--json` is `{"jobs": [...], "threads": [...], "total": {...}}`, waits in seconds, null when nothing was answered.
- `done [--report-file <file>] [--abandon]`, workers only: the message names the report file as an absolute path. With `FLEET_ISSUE` set, `--report-file` is required and comes first: `atb linear comment`, then `atb linear release --agent $FLEET_AGENT --reason done --done` (`--reason abandoned --abandon` with `--abandon`), then delivery naming the issue. - `worker end <name> [--report-file <file>]`, for a worker that cannot run `done`: the lead of its job, a thread agent or a caller without `FLEET_ROLE` (a worker, a lead of another job, a row that is not a worker, and a watch worker are refused, exit 1; no live row, exit 4). Its worktrees are the job's recorded worktrees holding the cwd its row records; another agent with its cwd inside one, or what `job end` would refuse to lose there, is exit 5 listing it, nothing changed. With a work order on the row: `atb linear comment` (the report, else a fixed line naming the caller, `FLEET_AGENT` or `a shell`), then `atb linear release <issue> --agent <name> --reason abandoned --abandon`, each a recorded step under `worker-end:<agents.id>`, so a retry skips the done ones. Then the tab of the pane its row records is closed as `thread end` closes its own (whether or not herdr lists an agent there), its worktrees removed as `job end` removes them but with their branches kept (`kept branch <branch>`: commits not pushed stay for the lead; marked removed in `worktrees`), its row ended; prints `ended worker <name> of job <job>`. The job's other worktrees stay. A caller that is not the row's parent (`FLEET_AGENT` differs) then tells the parent with `herdr agent prompt`, headed `[FROM: <caller>]` (`a shell` without `FLEET_AGENT`): who ended the worker, its work order released as abandoned, the branches the ledger records for its worktrees, the report file; `told <parent>` on success. The worker is ended by then, so a parent gone, blocked or giving no clear signal (or herdr failing) is one `warning:` line on stderr, the exit code unchanged.
- `job end [<job>] --report-file <file> [--abandon] [--close-parent done|abandoned]`, the job's lead only (`<job>`, when given, must be `FLEET_JOB`): refused (exit 1) while the job has live workers, naming them. With `FLEET_ISSUE`: `atb linear comment <issue> --body-file <report>`, then `atb linear release <issue> --agent <lead> --reason done --done` (`--reason abandoned --abandon` with `--abandon`); with a parent issue: `atb linear comment <parent> --body-file <conclusion>`, then `atb linear release <parent> --agent <lead> --reason 'job <job> ended' --restore`, which gives back the claim `job start` took and returns the parent to the state its claim comment recorded (`from:`), so a later job can take it; `--close-parent` closes it, with the release `--done` or `--abandon` as above, and so does a job ending done without it when `--new-parent` created the parent (`--done`: nothing else asked for that parent). atb failing the `--restore` (no `from:` on the claim, the state gone) is exit 5 like any atb step. `--abandon` is the job's own outcome; on the parent it only keeps a created parent from closing as done; `--close-parent` on a job without a parent issue, or with another value, is refused (exit 1). The conclusion is `Job <job> ended: <done|abandoned>.` then `Lead: <lead>. Work order: <issue>. Report: <absolute path>` (no work order: that clause omitted); it is printed on stdout after `ended job <job>: <outcome>, <n> rows ended`. With a home thread it is delivered there as `ask-thread` delivers (headed with the lead's name, marked a conclusion), by the recorded `conclude` step. An atb step exiting non-zero stops it: exit 5 naming the step, nothing changed in herdr; running it again continues from the first step not recorded, including after the ledger update (the job already ended) when the workspace close failed. Something left in the way is exit 5 listing it, the job still open. `job end <job> --force` from a thread agent or a shell without `FLEET_ROLE` (a lead or worker is refused): no report file, no `--abandon`, no `--close-parent`; closes the workspaces labelled `<job>`, removes the job's directories, ends its live rows and marks an open job `abandoned`; prints `reclaimed job <job>: 0 left, <n> rows ended`, then `Linear steps not done for job <job>; finish them by hand:` with one `  - ` line per step not recorded (work order comment and release when the lead has one, parent comment and release when the job has one); a job with nothing left ends 0 rows, exit 0.
- `worktree <repo> [--name <name>] [--branch <branch> | --detach <ref>]`: needs `FLEET_JOB`; makes `<worktrees>/<repo>/<job>[-<name>]` on a new branch `<job>[-<name>]` (or `--branch`) from origin/HEAD of the checkout `<checkouts>/<repo>` or, when there is none and `<repo>` is `<initiative prefix><I>`, `<initiatives>/<I>` (else exit 1 naming both), without tracking, or with `--detach` as a detached checkout of `<ref>` (resolved in the checkout after `git fetch origin`; no branch, so `job end` deletes none), and prints only the path; a new worktree whose root has `statute.toml` gets `statute compile --private-only` run there when `statute` is on PATH (the private output statute writes is untracked, and this compile touches no tracked file; fleet names no statute path and passes its output to stderr), a missing `statute`, a failed compile or one past 60 s (killed with its process group) only warning on stderr; `job end` finds the checkout a worktree came from with `git rev-parse --git-common-dir`. ## Known issues and next steps

The judge is green against the Go binary and runs in CI. `watch`'s screen filter knows only Claude Code's screen; its judge arms step the clock through `FLEET_WATCH_NOW` and wait 2 s between runs so a moving agent's screen moves in real time too. A thread fednet can no longer give (deleted, say) has its rules skipped on every run, each saying so and exiting 5, while it has a live agent, an open job or a pending question; nothing closes a thread agent's question pending there, or a lead's question moved there. The 45 s budget is checked between leads, between threads, and between watches and their subjects, so one thread agent started for a quiet thread, one watch worker, or a command source taking its 30 s can take a run past a minute. A watch taken out of the config keeps its subjects' rows, so one added later under the same name goes on from their counts. `watch`'s own posts (reminders, the closing lines) are messages in the thread, so they start a new quiet spell and push back `thread_idle`. A person's message during the 10 minutes a session is given to end does not cancel the reclaim. `runtime.NumCPU` honors the affinity mask, not a cgroup CPU quota, so the resource check in a quota-limited container compares the load with more CPUs than the quota allows. A delivery with no clear signal (exit 2) leaves the new agent's row `starting`, so the row does not claim a task the agent may not have; `job end --force` ends it. A lead's `job end` has no rollback: a step that fails mid-way is run again on the retry, so a comment can appear twice; and `atb linear release` is not itself resumable (its comment written, its state update failed: the retry is refused with status 4), which fleet does not repair; `job end --force` then finishes locally and names the step. `job end` ends the lead's own pane with the workspace, so the lead's last action is this command. An `inbox` run killed after its agent started is finished by fednet's retry (the row is `starting`, the full first message is delivered then); a retried exit 2 may deliver a message twice. A thread agent that vanished from herdr is ended by the next `watch` run (with a socket configured), or by `inbox` at the thread's next message, which ends the row without a closing line; an open progress card stays open either way. A session that ran without its ticket leaves no `Session <n> started` comment, and a ticket `thread end` creates has a generic description; a kept summary waits on the machine that ran the session, and is written twice when its file cannot be removed after Linear took it. A create that failed after Linear made the issue (atb exiting non-zero, or timing out) is not recorded, so a retry or a later session can make a second ticket. A spawn's claim that timed out after Linear took it is listed as claimed by nobody, and the worker's row is ended all the same. The redaction of atb's stderr knows Linear's key shapes, `Bearer` values and the key in `LINEAR_API_KEY`, not a key that `LINEAR_API_KEY_CMD` prints in another shape. The judge has no systemd: a fake `systemctl` starts the session, so the real unit is checked only by hand (`docs/knowledge/fednet-hook.md`). The fednet client's hook setup is in `docs/knowledge/fednet-hook.md`.

A home thread opened for a job started without a role is written to the ledger only after `fednet client open-thread` returns: a process killed in between leaves a thread in Slack with only fleet's opening message, and the next ask opens another. A lead or worker whose start stopped at a screen is not finished by `watch`: once past the screen it waits at its input box without its first message, its row `starting`, and only its starter is told. A watch worker's question reaches the thread on the next `watch` run, not at once, and the next worker for its subject starts on the run after a person's message there, since any message marks every pending question answered; the `unblock` watch's worker acts on it only when it plainly answers its screen's question. A watch worker reads the worker text, whose paragraphs on `fleet send` and `fleet done` are a lead's worker's; one paragraph tells it they are not its own, and a scope's `prompts.worker.append` reaches it too. A watch worker's question about a subject with a job is closed when that job ends, and the subject is then acted on by its text and `again` alone. The rules and the hands-off phrases know only Claude Code's screens, and the phrases are matched below the last horizontal rule, checked only against the screens in the tests.

Approval cards are not supported yet: `ask-human --approval` is refused, and `inbox` ignores payloads of type `approval`. The questions fleet itself posts (a watch worker's, a screen's, watch's at a `max`) are still plain text, answered by any message a person posts in the thread; making them cards is a follow-up. A snoozed card comes back as a new message, but the ledger keeps the first permalink, which then leads to the old card, which points down to the new one. A ledger at schema 16 is refused by an older binary, so going back needs the ledger from before. A reminder posted before the hub recorded where its posts go cannot be deleted (fednet exits 1, counted as deleted); one whose delete fednet refused with exit 2, as a hub older than its client does and as the new binary does in the seconds before its client process is handed over during an upgrade, stays in the thread for good, one message more; and one whose delete is retried is retried only while its thread has something watch acts on; how the mention looks and notifies in Slack is checked only against fakes.
