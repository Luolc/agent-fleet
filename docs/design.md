# fleet design

## What this is and is not

A CLI that runs and coordinates coding agents in herdr; not an agent itself, not a service, not a Go library.

## Model

Three roles. A **thread agent** (`thread-<slug>`, role `thread`) is one conversation on a Slack thread; it starts jobs and is the only agent that talks to people. A **lead** (`<job>-lead`) runs one job and starts workers. A **worker** has the fewest powers: it starts no agent, asks no person itself and ends no job. A lead's worker (`<job>-<name>`) does one task for its lead; a **watch worker** (`watch-<watch>-<key>`; a key not made of `[a-z0-9-]`, or a name past 32 characters, is cut and hashed) is started by `watch` for a subject of a `worker` watch (below), with no job and the parent `watch`, in the scope's `threads` workspace and in `$XDG_STATE_HOME/fleet/<scope>-watch/`, a directory fleet makes outside every checkout. No job name is `watch` or starts with `watch-`, `unblock-` or `revisit-` (the names of the screen helpers and revisit agents `watch` started before its agents were workers), so these names are never those of a job's agents. Each role's first message starts with fleet's built-in text for it (`thread_prompt.md`, `lead_prompt.md`, `worker_prompt.md` in `internal/cmd`, placeholders filled by fleet), then `common_prompt.md`, the text the three share (what the `FLEET_*` variables mean, the trust model, the `[FROM: <agent>]` header, making agents only through fleet, reporting at once a move of its own session to another model or a stop by a safety classifier, what needs a person's yes left to the repos' rules), then the additions (`common`'s, then the role's), each as the scope's config makes it (`prompts`, below), so fleet's mechanism reaches the agent with its task. The texts hold only what an agent needs before it reads anything else: who it is, what its role is for, the trust model, the hard limits and the formats it must recognize. How a role uses fleet is in the user-level skill `fleet` (`SKILL.md`, then `references/thread.md`, `lead.md` or `worker.md`), which each role's text points at; each command's flags, output and exit codes are in its `--help`, which matches the binary. They carry no policy (model tiers, review, merging, releases, what a public repo's text may name, the language people and tracker text are written in, what a report holds, the other skills to read) and restate no rule: that is the config owner's, and reaches agents through the owner's rules, the repo's `AGENTS.md` (with `AGENTS.override.md` and `CLAUDE.local.md` where present), which the lead and worker texts point at, and `prompts.<name>.append`. What needs a person's yes is left to those rules, with no list of fleet's own; agents, tabs, panes and worktrees are made only through `fleet`. The config is read at each start, and a running agent keeps the prompt it started with. A thread agent waits for a person on a lead's question only when the rules need a person's yes for it, and hands the rest back to the lead. Thread agents are started by `fleet inbox`, the fednet client's hook, when a message arrives on a thread without a live agent; they run in the herdr workspace `threads` of their scope's session (started by `inbox` through the systemd user unit `fleet-scope@<scope>` when it is not running and the thread gets an agent), one tab per thread next to a `shell` tab that runs no agent (pane `<scope>-shell`; both made or restored before each start), in the directory of the thread's channel (below). A **thread** has at most one live agent and, with a Linear team configured for the scope, one thread ticket for its whole life; each conversation on it is a session, counted on the thread's row, ended by `fleet thread end`. A session lasts while the thread waits on a person or a job (a pending question in it, or an open job whose home thread it is): the answer and the lead's messages reach the live agent, an idle session costs nothing, and a new one starts cold with the prompt, the summaries and the thread.

A **job** is the unit of work, named by its id, with at most one parent issue in Linear. It is a row in the `jobs` table from `job start` until `job end`; its lead and workers are rows in `agents`. A thread agent starts a job, and so may a caller without `FLEET_ROLE` (a timer, a shell) for a single-repo job, under the name `fleet`; such a job has no home thread until its lead first asks a question. A job is single-repo (`--repo`: the lead runs in the main checkout `<checkouts>/<repo>`, or for an initiative's repo `<initiative prefix><I>` with none, in `<initiatives>/<I>` as `fleet worktree` finds it; read-only by convention) or cross-repo (the lead runs in `<job>/` inside the checkout of its home thread's initiative, `<initiatives>/<I>/`, or the general initiative's for a thread of one repo; fleet makes and removes `<job>/`, never the checkout). No command makes a worktree for an agent; agents open theirs with `fleet worktree`, and a worker runs wherever its lead's `--cwd` says.

A **scope** is one fleet: the herdr session `fleet-<scope>`, one ledger and one settings file, isolated from the other scopes on the machine. The caller's scope is `--scope`, else `FLEET_SCOPE`, else `main`; `inbox` takes it from the message (the hub's per-channel `scope`, `main` when absent). The repo of a job is a job attribute, not the ledger key. A scope's own settings (what no repo can hold: the Linear team of thread tickets, the fednet socket, the Slack workspace's address, the machine's directories, the channel names and the load multiplier) are in `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/fleet/<scope>.json`.

A **channel** decides where a thread belongs (its *mapping*), by the scope's settings (below): `<repo prefix><R>` runs its thread agents in the main checkout `<checkouts>/<R>`; `<initiative prefix><I>` in `<initiatives>/<I>/`, the checkout of the initiative's repo, which has the channel's name; a direct message to the bot belongs to the general initiative, `<initiative prefix><general initiative>`. fleet makes none of these checkouts. Any other channel is ignored. A thread keeps the mapping and directory recorded at its first delivery, but its kind (repo or initiative) is read with the current prefixes, so `channels` is changed only while no thread is recorded.

## Parts and how they connect

- `cmd/fleet`: parses the command line (standard `flag`, one `FlagSet` per command, `--scope` global, `job` (with `start`, `list` and `end`) and `thread` command groups) and dispatches to `internal/cmd`; turns a failure into `fleet: <message>` on stderr and the exit code.
- `internal/cmd`: one file per command, plus `agent.go` with what the two starters share (names, the task file, the config and Linear settings of a job, the work order, places in herdr, starting the agent and getting it to its input box (a small table by herdr kind gives each runtime's start arguments, input-box test, trust key and watch filter): Claude Code past the folder-trust dialog, only the dialog for the agent's own directory, one key per press from where the cursor is among the dialog's options on the screen just read, not on the shell's command line above it; Pi with `--approve` and no key, its screen polled until its own editor shows (herdr's Pi manifest knows only working, so a dialog reads as idle); a start that fails with herdr's `pane get` showing no agent in the pane (the agent exited, as a PATH shim does while an update reinstalls Claude Code, or a gateway entry refusing the session) is made once more in the same pane after 3 s, a slow one still in the pane is not; delivery, the machine's resources). `job start` runs its checks, does the Linear steps (parent created with `--new-parent`, parent claimed for the lead, lead's work order created and claimed), writes the job row and the lead's `starting` row, brings the main checkout the lead reads (the repo's, or the initiative's of a cross-repo job) up to origin (below), makes the cross-repo job's directory and the workspace, starts the lead and delivers its first message: the lead prompt, `## Note from fleet` when that checkout was left as it was or the start is `--light`, the latest message a person posted in the home thread (from the thread's row, as one JSON line with its sender and time) and the task. `spawn` (lead only) does the same for a worker in a tab of the job's workspace, with the job's parent issue from the jobs row, its first message the worker prompt, `## Note from fleet` with `--light`, and the task. `send` adds the `[FROM: <agent>]` header and delivers through `herdr.Prompt`, reading a Pi target's screen first (kind from `agent get`) and typing nothing, exit 3, unless it shows Pi's editor; a Claude Code target (kind `claude` or none) that herdr does not find working has its transcript read first, from its start for at most 5 s, as the watch `model-changes` reads it (Models, below): one stopped by a refusal with no reply since that ended its turn, and not waiting at its prompt after a stop with no fallback, is paused on Claude Code's `Session paused` dialog, which text typed in or its Enter would answer, so it gets nothing typed and no key, exit 8, its screen not printed (the watch `model-changes` replaces it only as Models below says, once and when herdr finds it idle or done, so the text tells the sender not to wait in a loop: a lead ends its own worker with `worker end`, anyone else tells whoever started it, and one started by the paused session itself ends its turn and waits, the watch having told the people in that session's thread), while one fleet cannot tell about (no pane, session or transcript, a read that failed or did not end) gets the message as before; a thread agent's message that reaches a lead marks that lead's questions pending in the thread agent's thread answered, but those a pending card of the thread agent's asks the people for. `done` (workers only) delivers the report to the lead and ends the caller's row only when herdr reports it delivered; with `FLEET_ISSUE` it first writes the report to that issue and releases it through `internal/atb`, each step recorded under its live row's `worker-end:<id>`, so a `done` run again after a failed delivery, `worker end` or a reclaim does not repeat them. `worktree` opens `<worktrees>/<repo>/<job>[-<name>]` from origin/HEAD, or with `--detach <ref>` as a detached checkout of that commit (for a review of a PR head), and records it in the scope's ledger. `job end` (the lead, from its own pane; refused while the job has live workers) writes the report to the lead's work order and releases it, writes the conclusion to the parent issue and releases it, closes the other agents of the job's workspace, refuses while any agent but the lead has its cwd inside a recorded worktree or the cross-repo directory, removes them, ends the job with its outcome and the lead's row, hands the conclusion to `concluded` (the home-thread hook; it prints until thread agents exist) and closes the workspace last. `job end <job> --force` (a thread agent, or no `FLEET_ROLE`) reclaims a job from outside: no report; it closes the workspace first, cleans up with the same checks, ends the job as `abandoned` with its live rows, then does the Linear steps of the reclaim (below). `status` prints the open jobs as `job list` does, then the live rows joined with `herdr agent list`, then (without `--job`) the orphan tabs of the `threads` workspace (below, under `watch`). `report` reads only the ledger's `jobs`, `threads` and `questions`. `job list` joins the open jobs with their live lead and workers. `watch` (`watch.go`, `watchthread.go`, `watches.go`; for a timer, one run per scope at a time under a lock file next to the ledger, starting nothing new after 45 s) reads everything first (herdr, the screens, the config files, with a socket each relevant thread's newest message through `fednet client read-thread`; the keys of the config it no longer uses are named on stderr), a failed read doing nothing (but a thread fednet answers it cannot give, its exit 1 or 2, only skips that thread's rules, saying so, exit 5), then acts, sending as `watch` through `send`'s path. Jobs: each live lead's and job worker's herdr status, `state_change_seq` and a hash of its screen with the spinner, input box and footer stripped (Claude Code's or Pi's, by the kind `agent list` gives) are recorded; one unchanged for the job's `worker_stale` (or gone from herdr: a `starting` row only 10 minutes after its start, since a start writes the row before herdr has the agent), and not blocked, is a suspect; every suspect is printed, and by the watch `stale-workers` (below) each lead in herdr is told only when the set of suspects among its workers changes; a lead's own state goes into the quiet-thread notice. Gone (`watchgone.go`): an active lead or job worker gone from herdr (missing from `agent list` and no agent in the pane its row records, so an `agent list` entry watch cannot read ends nothing; a `pane list` entry without its ids fails the read) has `gone` recorded as its status, `last_change_at` then the time it went; 10 minutes on (two more runs of a 5-minute timer, so a herdr restart or one listing that missed it ends nothing; a constant, not a setting) its row is ended. A worker's work order first gets a line saying so and is released as abandoned (steps `watch-gone:<id>`, as `worker end` does, since `worker end` takes only live rows; a step its `done` recorded under `worker-end:<id>`, its delivery then failed, is not done again), and its lead, when in herdr, is told; a lead's job stays open and the agent of its home thread, through `route`, is told once (the row it is told about is ended), to decide on `job end --force`. Then the tab of each ended lead's or worker's pane that `herdr pane list` still has under the agent's name is closed, unless a live row records that pane. Thread tabs (`threadtabs.go`): a thread agent's row still `starting` 10 minutes after its start, missing from `agent list` with no agent in its pane, is ended; then a tab of the `threads` workspace, other than its `shell` tab, whose panes no live row records (an **orphan tab**) is closed only when it has one pane, the newest ended thread row recording that pane is of the thread whose slug is the tab's label, herdr sees no agent in the pane, and the pane's shell is its foreground alone with no child process (`/proc/<pid>/task/*/children`); and two runs 10 minutes apart or more found it so in the same tab with the same shell, as did each run between them that read it (`orphan_tabs`). Each close is a line on stderr, nothing is posted; an orphan tab no ended thread row records is never closed, and `status` lists every orphan tab with when watch closes it or why it does not. Workers: then `watchworkers.go` closes the watch workers that are done (below). Screens: then `unblock.go` looks at the agents stopped at a screen (below). Models: then `watchmodels.go` reads the live agents' Claude Code transcripts (below). Threads, with a socket: a live thread agent gone from herdr has its session ended as broken off (the claim its session took released `--restore`, the ticket back to its state before that claim, or, atb refusing that with exit 1, released keeping the state); a thread idle for `thread_idle` has its session reclaimed (the agent asked to end, then after 10 minutes ended by `thread end`'s recorded steps with a comment that no summary was left); by the watch `quiet-threads` a thread with no pending question quiet for `thread_quiet` is asked about once per quiet spell (the newest message's ts kept in `threads.quiet_asked`): with open jobs reporting to it, its thread agent, started through `route` when none is live, gets each job's state; with none, a live agent is asked why it has not ended. Questions: one post reminds a thread of its pending questions (a card by its link; a snoozed card not until it is back, its reminders and timeout then counted from `snooze_until`) but the leads' (which went to the thread agent, never to the people; the live thread agent, unless being reclaimed, gets one message listing them, but those a pending card asks the people for, at the same steps, counted only when not surely undelivered (blocked, not found), a moved one's marked as its job's ended, none being started for it) at each of `reminders` after the oldest (the count and the post's msg_id on the questions), mentioning `threads.last_user` (`-mention`; none when empty, and posted again without it when fednet refuses the mention: exit 2 from a fednet too old for it, 3 for a user not on the hub's list), and replaces the reminder before it: once the new one is posted the older ones are `stale` and deleted (`fednet client delete`), exit 1 (gone) counting as deleted, 4 and 5 (hub unreachable, not in Slack yet) or a fednet that did not run tried again next run, any other exit (a hub too old for it) leaving it `kept`, each failure said on stderr without stopping the other rules; a thread agent's question pending for `thread_question` is closed and its session reclaimed; a lead's pending for `lead_question` (counted, while a card asks the people for it, from that card's `snooze_until` too) gets the lead told, and 30 minutes later the job, if still open with the question pending, is reclaimed as `job end --force` does (`reclaimJob`) and the Linear steps not done are posted to the thread with the command that retries them, its pending questions closed; so are those of a job that ended after its lead was told (`jobs.reclaim_at` set), by the lead or by a run that stopped before closing them, and a screen question of any job that ended, its agent gone with the job. A lead's question still pending when its job ended any other way (`job end`, `job end --force`) is moved to the home thread, keeping its row (`job`, `asked_by`, `asked_at`, the reminder count): a question belongs to the open job of its name only when asked after that job started, so a later job of the same name does not take it. It is the thread's from then on: its thread agent reminded of it as of any lead's question, it keeps `thread end` waiting, and the live thread agent, unless being reclaimed, is told of it once (step `watch-moved:<id>`), none being started for it; it is closed after `thread_question` from when it was asked, without reclaiming the session, which may be doing other work.
- `internal/cmd/inbox.go` and `thread.go`: `inbox` reads the event file, finds the thread's mapping from `trigger` and `channel_name` (an ignored channel opens no ledger unless the scope's ledger already knows the thread), reserves the `msg_id` in the scope's ledger (a delivered or dropped message exits 0 at once; a `typed` one, whose delivery gave no clear signal, is never typed again: while its agent is in herdr the retry presses Enter once when the input box still holds it, as `Deliver` does, and takes an empty box as submitted, else it is routed again), and routes: a live thread agent gets the message through `Deliver` headed `[FROM: inbox]`, at work or not (Claude Code queues it) (a person's message marks answered only the thread's pending questions an older fleet posted as text, and a lead's whose job ended, unless a pending card asks the people for it); a message delivered or given to a new agent is recorded on the thread's row as the latest from a person (`last_text`, `last_user`, `last_ts`); a live row whose agent herdr no longer has is ended, and the tab of the pane it records is closed when herdr sees no agent in that pane and it is the tab's only pane (else, or when the close fails, a note on stderr, and the message goes on); otherwise, when the thread's directory exists (else one line is posted and the message dropped, as for Linear below), `threadStart` reserves the agent's row and counts the session, does the Linear steps (new thread: ticket created with `atb linear create --label thread` and claimed; known thread: claimed again, a `Session <n> started` comment, the `Session <n> ended` comments read with `atb linear query`), brings the thread's directory up to origin, makes the tab in the `threads` workspace (the workspace when missing), starts Claude Code as `spawn` does (a start that fails with no agent left in its pane ends its row and closes its tab, so the next try starts afresh; one whose agent runs stays `starting` for the next try to finish, which does not take an agent at work back to its input box; one whose first message gave no clear signal turns `active`, the message typed in) and delivers the embedded prompt (`thread_prompt.md`) with the channel context, the summaries, `## Note from fleet` (what Linear did not do, a checkout left as it was) and the message. Linear (`linear.go`): a step atb fails is tried twice more after 3 s and 7 s, but not a call that timed out or could not run, nor a claim another agent holds (atb's exit 3), and no try begins, nor a pause is waited out, once the steps' shared 90 s are spent (`LinearBudget`), so, a call taking at most atb's 60 s, the Linear part of a start ends within 2.5 minutes; each failed try is a JSON line in `<scope>-linear.log` (below). A claim a try left with exit 5 (atb 0.4.0 or later: written, its conflict check not finished) is on the ticket even when a later try fails another way, so the session keeps it and the agent is told, unless a later try lost to an earlier claim (exit 3, which releases it); a last try that exits 5 is logged `unchecked`. A step that still fails ends the Linear steps and the agent starts anyway: after the claim it keeps the ticket and is told what is missing; before it (the ticket not created, Linear unavailable, or the claim held) the session runs without its ticket (`FLEET_ISSUE` empty, the row's `issue` too), the thread is told in one line, as far as fednet and the socket allow, naming the step and atb's exit or the holder, and the message is delivered as usual. The next session creates or claims the ticket as it would anyway; `thread end` of a session without its ticket in a scope with a Linear team first keeps the summary on the machine (`<scope>-summaries/<slug>/<session>.md` beside the ledger, 0600, step `keep`), then writes the thread's kept summaries to its ticket, creating the ticket (unclaimed) when there is none (step `create`), each file removed once Linear took it; what Linear does not take stays kept, the ending goes on, and the next start that claims the ticket writes the kept summaries, oldest first, before its `Session <n> started` comment; `watch` reclaims such a session with no Linear step, since the agent's row has no ticket. A main checkout (a thread's directory, a lead's) is changed only by `git merge --ff-only --no-overwrite-ignore` after `git fetch origin` (30 s), when it is on origin/HEAD's branch, has no change to a tracked file, and is only behind, so an untracked or ignored file the update would bring in stops it; otherwise, and when the fetch fails, it is left, the start goes on, and the agent's first message says why (git's own output left out). `thread end` is refused while the thread waits (its pending questions and the open jobs it is home to, listed) unless `--asked-to-end`; it then writes the summary comment, releases the ticket, posts the closing line (`会话已结束 · <ticket>`, a small grey `-footer` post that also completes an open progress card; the thread and ticket come from the ledger rows, not the environment), ends the row and closes the tab last, each step recorded through `runStep` (`steps.go`) so a retry continues and the line is posted at most once; `thread post` posts a thread agent's text to the thread its live row records, through the scope's socket, or with `--deliver --thread <thread>` (a key, or a Slack link to a message, read as its `thread_ts` or else its `p` segment) to that thread with `fednet client post -deliver`, the text signed `— <agent> · <its thread>`; a message of trigger `handoff` or `relay` (another machine's agent) is delivered or starts an agent as above, but headed `[FROM: <from_client>/<agent>]` and `[HANDOFF|RELAY from <from_client>/<agent> in thread <key>]`, the agent and key parsed only from a valid signature as the text's last line (labels the sender gives itself, never used for routing or permission; without one, the machine alone), never written to `last_*` and answering no question; `thread progress` sends the agent's whole progress card (or `--done`) the same way, keeping no card state (the hub tracks the one open card per thread and closes it before any post); `thread set-project` and `thread relate` act on `FLEET_ISSUE`. The embedded prompt names neither the thread key nor the socket: thread agents post only through fleet; it tells them to post one line on a person's message, keep a card while they work, and post the reply; the session summary has a section on what the people decided or corrected, and a job's task file carries what bears on it.
- `internal/cmd/askhuman.go`: a question for the people is always a card, and only a thread agent posts one. `ask-thread` (leads) records a lead's card as pending (table `questions`; the same card asked again while pending is the same row) and takes it to the job's home thread through `route` as `[QUESTION #<id> from <lead>]` and the card's JSON, so a thread agent is started for the thread when none is live; fleet posts none of it: the thread agent answers it with `send`, looks it up, or asks the people with `ask-human --card --for <id>`. The delivery is a step recorded under `ask-human:<question id>`, so a retry continues and never delivers twice. A single-repo job without a home thread gets one at the first ask, before the question is recorded: `fednet client open-thread` in the channel of its checkout (`<repo prefix><R>`, or the initiative's channel for an initiative's checkout), or in the general initiative's when fleet knows no id of that channel, the channel's id taken from the newest thread of it in `threads`, or else from the route `fednet client routes -json` gives with the channel's name, read once per open (direct messages, the `dm` route and ids starting `D`, left out; routes fednet cannot give name no channel, said on stderr); the thread opens with fleet's line naming the job and its repo (fednet opens no thread without a message), never the question; the new thread is written as a `threads` row of that mapping and as `jobs.home_thread` in one transaction. `ask-human --card` (thread agents) records the card and posts it with `fednet client ask -ref <scope>:<id>`, the ref carrying the scope since one fednet client serves the machine's scopes and an answer carries nothing but the ref; fednet alone defines and checks the card (its fields and limits: `fednet client ask --help`; the shapes fleet's agents write and receive are in `fleet ask-human --help`), and posts no second card for a ref, so a retry after a failure is safe; a card fednet refuses (exit 2) posted nothing and its question is closed. `--close` takes a card back (`fednet client ask-close`). `ask-human --file`, deprecated, keeps an older prompt's agents asking: a lead's text goes on as `ask-thread`, a thread agent's is posted as a card with no options. `closeCards` takes back, warning on failure, the card of every question fleet closes and the pending cards asked for it. `concluded` (`jobend.go`, the `conclude` step of `job end`) takes the job's conclusion to the home thread the same way, marked as a conclusion that needs no answer; a job without a home thread only prints it, and a delivery that did not succeed fails the step so a retry delivers again.
- `internal/cmd/handoff.go`: `thread handoff` (thread agents) hands a task to the channel of a repo or initiative, its own channel included (a new thread beside the caller's), or to a channel named with `--channel <name>`, where the task goes to a new thread agent; only the task moves, never a job. Before a thread is opened, the target's route in `fednet client routes -json` is read: a `machine` there other than this machine (`fednet client whoami`, fednet v0.9.0 or later) makes it a handoff to another machine; routes or a whoami fednet cannot give are exit 5, before anything is recorded; a route without a machine is this machine's. A target of this machine with neither of the scope's prefixes is refused (exit 1). A handoff to another machine opens the thread with `open-thread -owner route -from-thread <caller's thread>` (so fednet's hop count covers handoffs back and forth), its first message, which fednet delivers to that machine as trigger `handoff` and is all it gets, fleet's line, the task and the signature `— <agent> · <thread key>`, refused over 4000 characters before anything is recorded; the thread is recorded only as the row's `to_thread`, not in `threads`, which is how a rerun knows it is another machine's, and only the steps `post` and `mention` run: no local agent, no ticket relation, no marks. The target channel's id comes from `channelOf`, the lookup `ask-thread` uses for a home thread (the ledger's newest thread of the channel, else fednet's routes), so fleet opens no channel and changes no route. The handoff is a `handoffs` row; fleet opens the thread with `fednet client open-thread` (fleet's line naming the source channel as `<#<id>>`, the source thread, the task's title, taken as `inbox` takes a thread ticket's, and the agent) and records it as a `threads` row of the target and as the row's `to_thread` in one transaction, then runs the steps of `handoff:<id>`: `post` (one line to the source thread naming the target channel as `<#<id>>` and the new thread, first so the source thread shows it while the agent starts), `deliver` (through `route`, so the target's thread agent starts as from `inbox`, with its own thread ticket created and claimed, the task its first message as `[HANDOFF from <agent> in thread <key>]` headed `[FROM: <agent>]`, never written to `last_*`), `mention` (only with `--mention <user>`, which a thread agent adds when a person asked for the handoff themselves: `fednet client post -mention` in the new thread, so Slack notifies them there; posted once, a later run's `--mention` ignored; a mention fednet refuses fails the run, and a rerun without it finishes) and `relate` (`atb linear relate` of the two thread tickets, read from the ledger; while a thread has none, the step is said and left undone, the handoff unfinished, so a rerun relates them); the source ticket is not otherwise touched. A thread in either line is a Markdown link to `<fednet.slack_url>/archives/<channel>/p<ts without its dot>`, plain words without that setting; neither line shows a thread key. A rerun with the same task to the same target continues and opens no second thread.
- `internal/cmd/watchworkers.go` (run by `watch`): a watch worker is started in a new tab of the `threads` workspace, its first message, headed `[FROM: watch]`, the worker text (`common` and the additions as for a lead's worker; its slots filled for no job, the lead `watch`), `## Earlier` when earlier workers of the subject asked (their questions with their state and the last press on each card; for a question posted as text before cards, the latest message a person posted in the thread of the last), and `## Your task`: `fleet watch, watch `<name>`, subject `<key>`; your question file is <dir>/<worker>-question.md:`, the subject's text and the watch's prompt (slots `{{key}}`, `{{text}}`, `{{question_file}}`). Its row's `task` is the subject as JSON (`watch`, `key`, `job`, `thread`, `agent`). - `internal/cmd/watchmodels.go` and `watchtakeover.go` (run by `watch`, the default watch `model-changes`): fleet never switches a session's model, never types `/model` or any other command, and types into a session only its own messages. A change no fallback made, such as a person's `/model`, is only reported: replacing that session would undo a person's choice. A session neither has nothing done, so the events of a first-seen session's past, or of one switched back, are only reported. A stop or move is decided once (`transcripts.decided_at`, cleared once a read finds the session neither, even mid-read, so the next stop is a new one); a session still working (herdr's status not idle or done, its blocked screens left to the screen rules) or a row not active is looked at again next run. By role: a **lead or worker** gets its session replaced (`takeOver`): its first message is rendered as its start rendered it (the role's prompt from the current config, the lead's latest person's message, the statute and light notes, `## Your task` with the work order's URL and the task file the row records), headed `[FROM: watch]`, with a `## Note from fleet` on the takeover: the stop or move and the agent's latest recorded events (metadata only), what stays the same, its directory (whose branch, HEAD and `git status` it reads itself, so fleet adds no git call) and the files in its `FLEET_SCRATCH` whose names hold its name (paths only, names of a plain shape), and that the step that was stopped is not to be redone as it was or in other words but done another way or handed to its lead (a worker) or the people through `ask-thread` (a lead). The same row and name go on: the work order and parent issue stay claimed under that name (no claim, no release, R357), `FLEET_ISSUE`, the cwd and the parent are passed on, and messages reach the new session by the same name; it is the same attempt by the same agent, not another agent taking over an abandoned one (R178, R180), so no new work order. The steps: a new tab in the job's workspace (labelled as its start labelled it), then the old session's tab closed, then the row `starting` and the agent started in the new pane as `spawn` starts it, with the `kind`, `model` and `effort` the ledger recorded at its start (never a model read off the transcript), its first message delivered, the row `active`. Each is done once: the row's `takeovers` counts a replaced lead's or worker's session and a nudge, the thread's `takeovers` a thread's replaced session (so a thread's new sessions are not replaced in turn), each counted before anything changes, with the action first recorded as failed, so a run that stops mid-way reports it and does not try again. - `internal/cmd/mark.go`: the emoji (the **marks**) fleet keeps on a thread's root message, so people find threads in Slack (`has::question:`). Two axes. The state: `question` while a card posted in the thread waits for the people (`questions` pending with a `card_id`), else `hourglass_flowing_sand` while the thread has a live thread agent or is the home thread of an open job, else `white_check_mark`; a waiting card comes first, since it is what needs the people; exactly one of the three is fleet's at a time: the wanted one is added with `fednet client react`, then the other two taken off with `-remove` (fednet counts one already there, or already gone, as done). ## Invariants

Each invariant links to the test that guards it, or is marked untested.

- The CLI behaves as the judge (`judge/`) says: argv, exit codes, stdout/stderr, ledger rows, herdr state and screens. Guarded by `judge/run.sh`; every command's cases are in `judge/usage.sh`, `send.sh`, `lifecycle.sh`, `watch.sh`, `worktree.sh`, `thread.sh`, `watchthread.sh`, `watches.sh`, `unblock.sh`, `ci.sh` and `models.sh`.
- `inbox` is idempotent by `msg_id`: the message is reserved before anything else, a message once delivered or dropped is never acted on again, and a rerun while the thread's agent row is live (a run killed after the start) delivers to that agent instead of starting another; one live thread agent per thread is a partial unique index. Guarded by `cmd/fleet/thread_test.go`, `internal/db/db_test.go` and `judge/thread.sh`.
- An ending (`thread end`) can always be finished: a retry skips the recorded steps and continues, so the closing line goes to the thread at most once, and `--force` finishes the local cleanup when Linear or the post keeps failing, naming what is left. Guarded by `cmd/fleet/thread_test.go` and `judge/thread.sh`.
- A thread agent's session is not ended while the thread waits on a person or a job, unless the agent says the people asked it to end (`--asked-to-end`) or `watch` reclaims it. Guarded by `cmd/fleet/thread_test.go` and `judge/thread.sh`.
- A Linear step failing in `inbox` never drops the message or stops the start: the agent starts with what Linear did and is told the rest, each failed try logged with atb's exit and its stderr, credentials redacted by shape and by `LINEAR_API_KEY`'s value, never in an error, a post or a prompt. Guarded by `cmd/fleet/thread_test.go`, `internal/cmd/linear_test.go`, `internal/atb/atb_test.go` and `judge/thread.sh`.

## Known issues and next steps

The judge is green against the Go binary and runs in CI. Its container sees the host's load average, so every scope config it writes sets `resources.load_multiplier` to 1000 and no judge arm checks the load check, which only `internal/cmd/agent_test.go` guards; the memory check still runs there. `watch`'s screen filters know only Claude Code's and Pi's screens, Pi's from its source and one capture of Pi 1.2.0; Pi's editor test reads only the last two borders on the screen, so Pi's login dialog (opened only by a `/login` typed into the editor) and an extension widget below the editor that draws a ─ border would be misread; its judge arms step the clock through `FLEET_WATCH_NOW` and wait 2 s between runs so a moving agent's screen moves in real time too. A thread fednet can no longer give (deleted, say) has its rules skipped on every run, each saying so and exiting 5, while it has a live agent, an open job or a pending question; nothing closes a thread agent's question pending there, or a lead's question moved there. The 45 s budget is checked between leads, between threads, and between watches and their subjects, so one thread agent started for a quiet thread, one watch worker, or a command source taking its 30 s can take a run past a minute. A watch taken out of the config keeps its subjects' rows, so one added later under the same name goes on from their counts. `watch`'s own posts (reminders, the closing lines) are messages in the thread, so they start a new quiet spell and push back `thread_idle`. A person's message during the 10 minutes a session is given to end does not cancel the reclaim. `runtime.NumCPU` honors the affinity mask, not a cgroup CPU quota, so the resource check in a quota-limited container compares the load with more CPUs than the quota allows. A delivery with no clear signal (exit 2) leaves a new lead's or worker's row `starting`, so the row does not claim a task the agent may not have; `job end --force` ends it. A lead's `job end` has no rollback: a step that fails mid-way is run again on the retry, so a comment can appear twice; and `atb linear release` is not itself resumable (its comment written, its state update failed: the retry is refused with status 4), which fleet does not repair; `job end --force` then finishes locally and names the step. `job end` ends the lead's own pane with the workspace, so the lead's last action is this command. An `inbox` run killed after its agent started is finished by fednet's retry (the row is `starting`, the full first message is delivered then); the retry of a `typed` message takes an empty input box as submitted, so a message a person cleared from the box is lost; and a message typed into an agent at work whose Enter Claude Code dropped (an image's path in a person's text) waits in the box unseen, since herdr's prompt to an agent at work ends at once. A thread agent that vanished from herdr is ended by the next `watch` run (with a socket configured), or by `inbox` at the thread's next message, which ends the row without a closing line; an open progress card stays open either way. A session that ran without its ticket leaves no `Session <n> started` comment, and a ticket `thread end` creates has a generic description; a kept summary waits on the machine that ran the session, and is written twice when its file cannot be removed after Linear took it. A create that failed after Linear made the issue (atb exiting non-zero, or timing out) is not recorded, so a retry or a later session can make a second ticket. A spawn's claim that timed out after Linear took it is listed as claimed by nobody, and the worker's row is ended all the same. The redaction of atb's stderr knows Linear's key shapes, `Bearer` values and the key in `LINEAR_API_KEY`, not a key that `LINEAR_API_KEY_CMD` prints in another shape. The judge has no systemd: a fake `systemctl` starts the session, so the real unit is checked only by hand (`docs/knowledge/fednet-hook.md`). The fednet client's hook setup is in `docs/knowledge/fednet-hook.md`.

A home thread opened for a job started without a role, and a thread `thread handoff` opens, is written to the ledger only after `fednet client open-thread` returns: a process killed in between leaves a thread in Slack with only fleet's opening message, and the next ask or handoff opens another. A lead or worker whose start stopped at a screen is not finished by `watch`: once past the screen it waits at its input box without its first message, its row `starting`, and only its starter is told. A watch worker's question reaches the thread on the next `watch` run, not at once, and the next worker for its subject starts on the run after a person's press on its card. A card fednet refuses (a worker's question over a limit, or one of fleet's own whose screen or subject text is too long) is not asked: the worker's is dropped with an error on the run, and fleet's own is asked again on each later run that asks it, refused the same way while the text is the same. A watch worker reads the worker text, whose paragraphs on `fleet send` and `fleet done` are a lead's worker's; one paragraph tells it they are not its own, and a scope's `prompts.worker.append` reaches it too. A watch worker's question about a subject with a job is closed when that job ends, and the subject is then acted on by its text and `again` alone. The rules and the hands-off phrases know only Claude Code's screens (herdr never reports Pi blocked, so an active Pi row never reaches them; a `starting` one past its grace is read with Pi's editor test), and the phrases are matched below the last horizontal rule, checked only against the screens in the tests.

The watch `model-changes` rests on what Claude Code writes but does not document: the session file `<claude>/sessions/<pid>.json`, the transcript's place and its records (`model_refusal_fallback`, `model_refusal_no_fallback`, `stop_reason` `refusal`), as Claude Code 2.1.282 and 2.1.296 wrote them (read 2026-10-29); when they change, it finds nothing and says nothing, and only `judge/models.sh`, against records written as those versions write them, would notice. Claude Code's own wording for a stop ("stopped by a safety classifier") was not found in any transcript; the `refusal` stop reason is what is read. Whether the session file names the new session after `/clear` was not checked; if it does not, the watch goes on reading the old transcript. A stop is read from the assistant records and `model_refusal_no_fallback` only: a stop Claude Code writes in a record of another shape would not be acted on, only reported. That a refused turn is retried once and its next refusal pauses on an unrecorded dialog was read from Claude Code 2.1.296's code and one transcript (2026-10-29), and that a stop with no fallback ends the turn at the prompt from 2.1.282 transcripts (the refusal, `model_refusal_no_fallback`, a `<synthetic>` refused message, `turn_duration`; read 2026-10-29), and that herdr reads that dialog as idle from its Claude manifest (2026.10.11.1, `herdr agent explain` on a screen rebuilt from that code: no rule matches); were herdr to read it as blocked, the screen rules would take it, not this watch. `send`'s check before typing rests on the same reading and has these limits too: a session that pauses between the check and the prompt is typed into; one whose dialog a person answered with `Edit prompt and retry`, for which no record is known, reads as paused until its next reply, so it gets exit 8 and the watch replaces it; one herdr finds idle between a refusal and Claude Code's retry would get exit 8 for that moment; each delivery reads the whole transcript (0.4 s for 90 MB of made-up records, measured 2026-10-30), and one not read within 5 s is typed into unchecked. A session stopped mid-turn on another dialog herdr reads as idle would be acted on too. A takeover can take a run past its 45 s (two starts at most, as for a watch worker). A replaced session's turn that was mid-way when herdr last said idle is lost with its tab; what it wrote to disk stays. A message that lands after the second look and before the old tab closes (or the thread's session ends) is lost with it too; that window is the time to render the first message and open a tab. A thread's session replaced while it waits on a person or a job is ended all the same, as a reclaim is: the pending questions and the jobs stay the thread's and reach its next session. `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` is read from watch's environment, not the agent's. A watch worker's events have no parent to tell (`watch`) nor a job's thread, so they are only lines on stdout. A ledger at schema 23 is refused by an older binary, so going back needs the ledger from before.

A mark is put by whichever command or `watch` run gets there, with no lock between them: two at the same moment may leave on the root two state marks at once, or one other than the recorded one, until `watch` puts the state mark again (at most 10 minutes later, as long as the run's 10 threads reach it); `eyes` has no such second put. A thread fednet cannot react on (its root deleted, say) is tried again on every `watch` run, each a warning, and takes one of the run's 10. The marks are checked only against fakes.

Approval cards are not supported yet: `ask-human --approval` is refused, and `inbox` ignores payloads of type `approval`. A record answers later waits for its attempt, so after a re-run a caller passes `--attempt`. One watcher per run holds per machine: another machine's callers have their own.
